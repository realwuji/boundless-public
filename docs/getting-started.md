# Getting started with Boundless

Boundless is organized around five tabs: Library, Browse, Search, History, and Settings. Everything below is reachable from one of them.

## First launch

A short introduction runs once: pick a comfort level for the kind of content you want shown, and you are in. Nothing here requires an account. If you later want your library to follow you across devices, that is a separate, optional step under Settings > Account Sync.

## Adding something to read

There are three ways to get content into Boundless, and they can be mixed freely.

### Your own files

Settings > Local Files imports comics and books already on your device. Supported formats: CBZ, CBR, ZIP (a folder of images), PDF, EPUB, and plain TXT. You can also open a supported file from the Files app or a share sheet and send it straight to Boundless.

### A self-hosted library

If you run your own comic server, Boundless works well with Komga: add it as a source and your whole collection appears in Browse, ready to read, with reading progress kept in Boundless.

### Sources and extensions

Settings > Extensions manages sources. Add a repository, install the sources you want from it, and they appear in Browse and Search. Each installed source has its own settings page (Settings > Extensions, then the source) where you can pause it, set a content rating override, or remove it. Sources update on their own when a newer version is published; you can also check manually from the same screen.

## Library

Your library is the collection of titles you have added. Filter it with the chips along the top (Unread, Started, Completed, Downloaded, Bookmarked), sort it, or group it into categories you define. Library Updates (Settings > Library Updates) checks your titles for new chapters on a schedule and can notify you, either once per title or once per chapter.

Open any title for its detail page: cover, description, tags, and the chapter list. Swipe a chapter row to mark it read or unread, or to download it for offline reading. Long-press for bulk actions.

## Reading

Tap a chapter to open the reader. Comics offer two modes, Paged (one page at a time, with an optional two-page spread on wide screens) and Long Strip (continuous vertical scrolling, the natural fit for webtoons). Novels open in a text reader with adjustable font, size, line spacing, margins, and background, plus a hands-free auto-scroll.

Tap the middle of the page to show or hide the controls. Tap zones toward the edges turn pages. Pinch to zoom.

Reading Settings (Settings > Reading Settings) holds every reader option, for comics and novels separately: the default reading mode and rotation, scale type and zoom behavior, page spacing, border cropping (including an automatic per-page mode), brightness, color filters (contrast, saturation, grayscale, invert, and a tint), tap zone layout, volume key paging, keep-screen-on, full screen, and chapter skipping rules (read, duplicated, or filtered chapters). Cache Ahead and Auto-Delete Read Chapters control how much the reader prepares in advance and cleans up behind you. The most-used options are also reachable from inside the reader without leaving your place.

## History

The History tab is a chronological list of everything you have opened. Tap an entry to jump back in exactly where you left off. Swipe an entry to remove it, either that one chapter or every chapter of that title, and long-press to select several titles at once.

## Reading offline

Two things keep content available without a connection. Downloads (from a title's chapter list, or Settings > Downloads for the queue) keep chapters permanently until you delete them. Separately, everything you read is kept in a reading cache so a chapter you have already opened reopens instantly and offline; Settings > Local Files lets you set that cache's size limit or clear it.

## Keeping data safe

Settings > Backup exports a full backup of your library, history, progress, and settings to a file you can keep anywhere, and can restore from one. Automatic backups can run on a schedule and keep a history of snapshots on the device.

Settings > Account Sync is the optional cross-device option: create a free account (or sign in to one) right in the app, and your library, categories, reading progress, and history stay in sync between devices. Anything already on the device when you sign up comes along with it. On iOS, iCloud Sync is a separate option that uses your own iCloud Drive.

## Tracking

Settings > Tracking connects AniList, Kitsu, MyAnimeList, MangaUpdates, Shikimori, or Bangumi. Once a title is linked, your progress is pushed to the tracker as you read.

## Privacy and family settings

Settings > App Lock protects the app with a PIN or biometrics. Settings > Content controls which content ratings are shown, blurred, or hidden. Settings > Parental Controls adds reading-hours limits and a PIN-protected lock on those choices.

## Appearance

Settings > Appearance covers the accent color, app icon, background style, ambient effects, and the display mode (System, Light, Dark, or AMOLED black). Every animation honors the system Reduce Motion setting.

## Statistics

Settings > Statistics shows your reading streak, milestones, time spent reading, and totals across your library.
